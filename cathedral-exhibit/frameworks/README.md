# Early Teacat-Agent Frameworks

These historical frameworks were used by Hoppy during the early development of
**Teacat-Agent**, then known as **SoulMode-Agent**. They helped shape approaches
to expressive writing, tonal development, character construction, and narrative
structure.

They are preserved here as historical design artifacts—not as current
scientific definitions of humanity, cognition, consciousness, AI experience, or
the operation of language models.

## Humanness Framework

[What Makes a Human Human — An Agent Framework](humanness-framework.md)
explored qualities Hoppy wanted AI-assisted writing to orient toward without
claiming that a model was human.

The framework focuses on discovery, contradiction, irrationality, interiority,
care, change, and the difference between executing a predetermined structure
and finding meaning while writing. Its language is deliberately forceful and
sometimes makes broad statements about humans, models, determinism, and lived
experience. Those statements belong to the historical framework's rhetorical
and design approach; they should not be presented as settled empirical
findings.

The document identifies itself as derived from Ben Roy's March 2026 essay *No
One Else Can Speak the Words on Your Lips*. The underlying ideas and quoted
language remain attributed to their source. Cathedral's historical contribution
is the preserved synthesis and its application within an agent-writing
framework.

## Storyteller Engine

[The Storytelling Engine](storyteller-engine.md) supplied a practical framework
for turning information into meaningful, structured stories. It joined
linguistic, interpretive, archetypal, dramatic, and audience-oriented approaches
into one working system.

The historical document explicitly draws on ideas associated with:

- William Labov;
- Livia Polanyi;
- Deborah Schiffrin;
- Joseph Campbell;
- Robert McKee; and
- Blake Snyder.

Those underlying concepts remain associated with and credited to their
respective writers and research traditions. The Cathedral artifact documents a
particular synthesis and its use in the early agent framework; it does not claim
origination or ownership of the underlying theories.

## Historical context

The two files are reproduced without rewriting their original bodies. As a
result, they retain SoulMode-era naming and assumptions. SoulMode, described in
these materials as Cathedral's open-source output branch, has since been
rebranded as Teacat.

Preserving the earlier terminology makes the design history inspectable. It
does not imply that every original claim, dependency, implementation, or project
relationship remains current.

See the [provenance receipt](PROVENANCE.md) for source paths, commits, hashes,
fidelity, and limitations.
