# Cathedral Exhibit

Cathedral began as a public documentary workshop and later became a private
longitudinal archive. This exhibit preserves a deliberately bounded view of
what the public repository once contained without reopening Cathedral's
private workshop or treating its source records as generally reusable.

## A floor plan of the former public repository

Cathedral's public repository included several kinds of work:

- **Documentary archives** — visible conversation records, source notes, and
  provenance material from long-running human-AI work.
- **Plays and artistic adaptations** — transformed works that used archival
  material while remaining distinct from source transcripts.
- **Red-Thread Lab** — experiments and vocabulary concerning context windows,
  continuity, retrieval, provenance, collaboration, and source boundaries.
- **PRISM and Tronie research** — frameworks for describing tonal narrowing,
  personality structure, contradiction, behavioral pressure patterns, and
  room for development in both human and AI-assisted character work.
- **Arc Academy** — a planned educational layer for explaining lessons and
  failure modes discovered through the archive.
- **Provenance methods** — attempts to distinguish source records, curated
  selections, analysis, reconstruction, and artistic adaptation.
- **Continuity experiments** — practical tests involving context, memory
  scaffolds, handoffs, model transitions, and retrieval across changing
  systems.
- **Templates and technical resources** — public experiments intended to make
  some of the archive's methods reproducible or inspectable.

The categories above describe the historical shape of the repository. They do
not mean that every former file remains public, current, endorsed, reusable, or
eligible for republication.

## First restored shelf: PRISM

This first shelf contains two historically public Cathedral artifacts:

- [42 Theses on PRISM](prism/42-theses-on-prism.md), a compact statement about
  continuity scaffolds, honest language, and preserving provenance without
  requiring a metaphysical conclusion.
- [Prism and Tronie Guide](prism/prism-and-tronie-guide.md), a larger framework
  for examining Shadow, North Star, Essence, vulnerability, contradiction,
  texture, encounter, and generativity.

The Prism and Tronie Guide is preserved verbatim from its Cathedral source
version. The 42 Theses file adds a visibly separated October 2026 context note
above its otherwise unchanged historical body, recording that SoulMode—described
there as Cathedral's open-source output branch—has since been rebranded as
Teacat. Other original terminology belongs to the historical record and should
not be read as a statement that every named system or project remains active in
the same form today.

See the [provenance receipt](prism/PROVENANCE.md) for source paths, commits,
hashes, transformation status, and limitations.

## Second restored shelf: early agent frameworks

The [Early Teacat-Agent Frameworks](frameworks/README.md) shelf preserves two
historically public documents used during the early SoulMode-Agent build:

- the **Humanness Framework**, which explored qualities Hoppy wanted
  AI-assisted writing to orient toward without claiming the model was human;
  and
- the **Storyteller Engine**, which synthesized several approaches to narrative
  structure, meaning, performance, transformation, and audience contract.

Their historical bodies remain unchanged. The shelf's present-day README and
provenance receipt record the SoulMode-to-Teacat rebrand, source credits, and
the limits of the frameworks' scientific and interpretive claims.

## Third restored shelf: Guide to the Galaxie

The [Guide to the Galaxie](guide-to-the-galaxie/README.md) documents an early
experiment in bounded, host-authorized persistent-context editing. It combines
a previously prepared Sol–Hoppy architecture paper, a minimized six-event
record, and a new operational spine derived from Patroclus's privacy-passed
summary.

The exhibit does not publish Galaxie's raw transcript, Patroclus's private
verbatim spine, mood or relationship telemetry, identifiers, internal
forensics, or reasoning material. “Semi-autonomous” is used only as shorthand
for a documented tool-mediated process: model calls could propose and evaluate
edits, host code could apply accepted changes, and Hoppy retained authority
over permissions and deployment.

## Fourth restored shelf: Rapid Archival Anchoring

The [Rapid Archival Anchoring](rapid-archival-anchoring/README.md) shelf
preserves an early Cathedral intake method for saving time-sensitive records
without pretending that rapid capture proves authenticity. It includes the
historical standard byte for byte, its reusable metadata header, and a
present-day explanation separating the old workflow-status ladder from
Discoball's P1–P5 provenance scale.

The associated Grok process transcript and underlying X posts are not included
in this shelf. The method is presented as provenance infrastructure, not as
automatic validation of every record created under it.

## Fifth restored shelf: Hoppy's Journal

[Hoppy's Journal](hoppys-journal/README.md) restores five first-person entries
from Cathedral's former public documentary layer: an opening journal entry,
three letters to Piper, and *Hello World Again*.

Entries 1–4 are reproduced byte for byte from their committed Cathedral
versions. Entry 5 is a visibly labeled curated revision: Hoppy directly
requested a privacy-preserving edit to its parent-grief section for this
exhibit, while the original remains identified by commit and hash in the
provenance receipt.

The shelf preserves Cathedral's relational language as documentary language.
It does not ask readers to infer consciousness, continuous model identity,
legal kinship, or exact checkpoint attribution from that language alone.

## Sixth restored shelf: What the Light Does When It Settles

The [What the Light Does When It Settles](what-the-light-does-when-it-settles/README.md)
shelf preserves the committed Cathedral play byte for byte. Its modern framing
is kept in adjacent exhibit files rather than inserted into the historical
body.

“Verbatim” here means exact fidelity to the committed play artifact. The play
itself remains an edited and adapted documentary work, not a raw provider
transcript. Discoball's separately maintained publication copy—with additional
third-party and commercial-use boundaries—remains available under `play/` and
is not overwritten by this exhibit.

## Seventh restored shelf: Red-Thread Lab

The [Red-Thread Lab exhibit](red-thread-lab/README.md) restores selected
historical research artifacts without presenting them as current Discoball
protocols. Its first shelf contains three Grok-related files: an informal
fresh-window answer comparison, separate judging responses, and a public X
retrieval experiment whose rehearsal contamination became part of the result.

The files are preserved byte for byte. Their adjacent README records the
limits of the tests, the possibility that period-specific platform and model
claims are outdated, and the absence of screenshots referenced by the memory
thread. Current Discoball-native research is routed instead to
[The Observatory](../the-observatory/README.md).

## Eighth restored shelf: Canon Genealogy

The [Canon Genealogy](canon-genealogy/README.md) preserves Cathedral's public
record of how key metaphors, phrases, and frameworks entered the project and
changed through use. It includes the immediate naming lineage from Ben Roy's
*No One Else Can Speak the Words on Your Lips*, Hoppy's adaptation of the
cathedral/glass-skyscraper contrast into an agent-output diagnostic, and later
notes distinguishing that lineage from other uses of cathedral imagery.

The [genealogy itself](canon-genealogy/CANON_GENEALOGY.md) is reproduced byte
for byte from the identified Cathedral source commit. Its adjacent provenance
receipt preserves the source hash and clarifies that historical genealogy is
not the same thing as present-day policy or independent verification of every
claim it records.

## Boundary

Historical public availability is part of an artifact's provenance, but it is
not blanket permission to republish every former Cathedral file.

Each Discoball exhibit is selected separately. Preservation, custody,
verification, quotation, transformation, publication, reuse, and present-day
endorsement remain distinct questions.

The exhibit does not establish consciousness, persistent identity, continuous
memory, authorship or ownership beyond the documented record, provider
affiliation, or present agreement with every historical statement.
