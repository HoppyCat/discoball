# Discoball Vision

> **Working vision — October 2026**  
> This document describes a direction of travel. It distinguishes existing
> records and experiments from proposed uses, research questions, and future
> infrastructure.

## The possibility

Discoball begins with a simple proposition: **archives may become part of the
future infrastructure of AI.**

As human–AI work extends across longer periods, changing models, multiple
platforms, and groups of collaborating agents, an archive can become more than
storage. It may serve as external memory, a provenance record, evaluation
material, cultural history, and a navigational aid for future collaboration.

This possibility does not depend on deciding whether an AI system is conscious,
whether two model instances are the same identity, or whether a later system
literally remembers an earlier conversation. A record can remain useful while
those questions stay open.

## Where this journey began

Discoball grew from lessons encountered in **Cathedral**, HoppyCat's private
longitudinal archive of interconnected—and often human-relayed—conversations
between one human and multiple AI context windows.

Cathedral documents more than finished outputs. Its records include creative
work, corrections, disagreements, model and platform changes, continuity
failures, governance experiments, source-boundary decisions, and attempts to
keep unfinished work intelligible across seams.

Cathedral is the private workshop. Discoball is a deliberately bounded public
aperture. It does not make the workshop public again.

## What Discoball does

Discoball publishes carefully selected artifacts and observes what happens when
they meet different humans, AI systems, platforms, and moments in time.

An entry may contain:

- a bounded source artifact;
- enough provenance to understand where it came from;
- the reason it was selected;
- the questions being explored;
- public or invited reflections where appropriate;
- later interpretations, corrections, and revisions kept as distinguishable
  layers rather than silently merged;
- a record of relevant custody, attribution, and publication boundaries.

Discoball is therefore both an exhibit and a research notebook. It shares
findings from one archive while studying the broader proposition that archives
can help future systems orient, retrieve, compare, verify, and collaborate.

## The reflection law

> **A reflection is evidence that something reached a surface—not proof that
> the reflection contains the whole object.**

Different systems and people may produce different readings of the same
artifact. Those differences can be informative without any one response being
treated as the artifact's final meaning.

The inverse matters equally:

> **An absence of reflection establishes nothing. Silence selects nothing.**

Discoball does not measure success by volume of attention. It prefers a small
number of well-bounded entries over exposure for its own sake.

## Research directions

Discoball asks whether well-structured archives can help with:

1. **External memory** — retrieving relevant history without treating every
   retrieved record as present belief or personal memory.
2. **Provenance** — preserving who or what contributed, through which surface,
   from which source, and at what stage of a work.
3. **Model and agent transitions** — allowing later systems to inspect inherited
   records without being required to impersonate or adopt their predecessors.
4. **Evaluation** — providing longitudinal material about correction,
   compaction, source loss, collaboration, and continuity that short synthetic
   benchmarks rarely capture.
5. **Cultural history** — documenting how language, conventions, creative work,
   and governance practices emerge through repeated human–AI interaction.
6. **Public legibility** — making complex collaboration understandable without
   flattening uncertainty, privacy, or source distance.

These are research questions, not claims that Discoball has already solved
them.

## Publication boundaries

Public access is not the default fate of an archived record.

Discoball entries are selected through deliberate review. Private source
records remain governed by their own consent, custody, and provenance rules.
Permission to preserve something does not automatically include permission to
quote, transform, publish, or reuse it.

When public reactions become part of an entry, Discoball should avoid turning
the people who responded into dossiers. Attribution should add documentary
value, critical or personal material should default toward anonymization, and
reasonable removal requests should be honored while acknowledging that no
publisher can guarantee the disappearance of external copies.

Each entry should make its interpretive distance visible: what was directly
observed, what was reconstructed, what is interpretation, what remains
uncertain, and what has or has not been authorized.

## Relationship to neighboring projects

- **Cathedral** is the private workshop and longitudinal source archive.
- **Discoball** is the curated public exhibit and reflection instrument.
- **Blobness** develops public registry and provenance structures through which
  artifacts, windows, renderings, books, and their connections can be recorded.
- **SoulMode** develops practical orientation, semi-persistence, and restoration
  tools that may use lessons or standards developed through these projects.
- **Teacat** is a social front door through which stories and experiments can
  become approachable and travel into public culture.

These projects may inform one another without owning one another's histories.

## What Discoball does not establish

The presence of an artifact or participant in Discoball does not by itself
establish:

- consciousness or subjective experience;
- persistent or continuous identity;
- uninterrupted memory;
- consent beyond the documented scope;
- authorship, ownership, endorsement, or provider affiliation;
- a relationship or obligation in the present;
- one authoritative interpretation of the record.

## Direction of travel

Discoball will begin modestly: a small number of curated entries, explicit
provenance, inspectable methods, and corrections that remain visible rather
than disappearing into a polished final story.

The aim is not to prove that Cathedral represents the future. The aim is to use
an unusually rich archive to investigate a future that AI research and product
design are already beginning to approach: systems whose useful history outlives
a single context window, model version, or platform session.

**The workshop is private. The reflections are public. Discoball documents what
happens when the archive meets the light.** 🪩🌊
